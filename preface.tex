%personal motivation
Before you lies the master's thesis \textit{Confined Nanoparticle Diffusion
Imaging via iSCAT Microscopy}, written to fulfill the graduation requirements of the Master of Applied Science in Engineering Physics at the University of British Columbia in Vancouver, British Columbia, Canada. 

My interest in nanoparticle diffusion developed from prior nanoparticle research that I had worked on as an undergraduate thesis student at the Leslie lab. This study acted as a catalyst which inspired me to further investigate the physical laws and principles of confined single-particle diffusion. 

%background relevant to the study (in context of the project)
At the time of the thesis start, the iSCAT microscope was recently assembled. The parts were ordered by Dr. Eric Boateng with help from the Grant lab in the department of chemistry at UBC. The system was assembled by Dr. Erik Olsén. The software, written in LabView, was programmed and set up by Dr. Erik Olsén. 

The diagrams and plots in this thesis were illustrated by the author using \textsc{inkscape}, \textsc{python}, and the \textsc{matplotlib} library \cite{inkscape, python3, Hunter:2007}.

In this thesis, the experimental plan and study outline was developed by the author. Simulations in Chap. \ref{chap:langevinsimulations}, computational analysis method in Chap. \ref{chap:ZEstimation}, and experimental data in Chap. \ref{chap:expresult} are original work. Experimental data were acquired with help from Dr. Erik Olsén. Dr. Olsén aided in optical alignment, preliminary data analysis, and overall project guidance. The samples used in chapter \ref{chap:expresult} were selected by the author and formulation of the lipid-nanoparticles was carried out by Yao Zhang of the Cullis lab at UBC. 

I would like to thank Dr. Erik Olsén for project guidance, mentorship, and support during my time as a master's student, Dr. Cynthia Shaheen for support and management support, and all of my colleagues at the Leslie Lab and the department of Physics. Lastly, I would like to thank Dr. Sabrina R. Leslie for the continued support towards academic and professional development. Without these individuals, this thesis would have not been possible.
