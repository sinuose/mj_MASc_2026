In much of nature, the idea of size scales is widely discussed and investigated. From a basic understanding of physics, one understands that the forces an object feels are directly linked to their mass, or volume and mass density. For example, we can easily understand what will happen to a basketball if we throw it up into the air, as we are sitting on planet earth. However, when we leave the familar size scale, such as having a basketball the size of planet earth, or the size of a gas molecule, our intuition begins to show its cracks. 

In size regime of nanoparticles, that is between 20-100nm, many biological particles of utmost importance tend to reside. For instance, in cellular biology, proteins, vesicles, enzymes, and organelles fit between \SI{4}{\nano \meter} to \SI{10}{\micro \meter}. In this size regime, the forces which one feels largely differ from that of say a basketball. Inter-molecular forces between nearby molecules have a strong effect on particle postion, thermal kicks from atomic molecules induce a stochastic motion non-determininstically, and forces of hydrodynamic pressure induce frictions slowing the particle down.
