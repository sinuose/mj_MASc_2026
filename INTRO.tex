% In much of nature, the idea of size scales is widely discussed and investigated. From a basic understanding of physics, one understands that the forces an object feels are directly linked to their mass or volume and mass density. For example, we can easily understand what will happen to a basketball if we throw it up into the air as we are sitting on planet earth. However, when we leave the familar size scale, such as having a basketball the size of planet earth, or the size of a gas molecule, our intuition begins to show its cracks. 
It is easy to visualize normally sized objects that surround us day-to-day. When speaking about nano-scales, the effects of forces and interactions between nano-objects become many orders larger than those observed in everyday life. In the nano-world, atomic molecules are constantly in motion, exchanging kinetic energy in a chaotic way. The higher the temperature, the more chaos there is - thus, the world is very difficult to describe, predict, and conquer. 

Since every chemical reaction in biological and chemical world depends on this motion, it is extremely important to understand the rate of diffusion. This is the reason this subject has been studied by many physcisits, chemists and bioligists. 

In the realm of biological processes, there are many such examples involving the particles in the nano size regime. For example, viruses and vesicles are two examples of small molecules which have immense importance in the field. To investigate such systems, it is often the case that to maintain long measurements of the same particle, it is necessary to have some form of confinement. The full understanding of the behavior of these nanoparticles under confinement is thus important, and not complete.

%\section{Outline for Thesis}

The thesis conists of 8 chapters. Chapter 1 is an introduction to the topic of nanoparticles and their relevance in biological and chemical studies. Chapter 2 provides a description of the litearture of confined brownian motion over the last century. Chapter 3 describes the theoretical description of interferometric scattering microscopy (iSCAT) and physical limits that apply in optical theory. Chapter 4 discusses the application of Brownian motion and iSCAT in a biological context, which details regarding lipid-nanoparticles and why they need to be studied on the single-molecule level. Chapter 5 consists of investigations of confined diffusion using Langevin simulations. A simulated system was developed and the size-diffusivity relation in an electrostaically screened system was investigated. Chapter 6 describes the 3D iSCAT tracking technique developed during this work. The method overview and validation and explicitly developed and validated Chapter 7 describes the method applied to experimental data and utilizes various nanoparticle species, highlighting their varied diffusions. Chapter 8 concludes the world and provides a future outlook on the topic and steps which need to be advanced.
