\section*{Layman Summary}



In scientific manufacturing, such as plastics, pharmacology, and food science small molecules, known as nano-particles, are part of the product or procedure. In order to ensure that the output product is of high-quality or up to standard, one tends to want to measure the properties of these nanoparticles. However, due to their size being smaller than that resolvable by light alone, there is a need to develop new methods to do this. This thesis aims to develop one such method to measure three-dimensional particle position in space, and mathematically extract information from this position in time to better improve measurements of nanoparticle characteristics.

\section*{Abstract}
The understanding of thermal movement of paricles within a confined space has been studied extensively in order to understand biological, chemical, and physical processes. Although the theoretical understanding of this confined diffusion is well defined in literature, not much work has been done to understnad the effects of electrostatic and hydrodynamic contributions to the size measurements measured from the motion of nanoparticles, as measurements are often instrinsically performed under confinement. This is due to the fact that 3D particle-tracking is not as commonly used as other, higher throughput sizing techniques. By employing developed techniques of nanoparticle tracking in tandem with a full theoretical model, the non-trivial effects of confinement on size-diffusivity were directly investigated and characterized. The work described in the thesis advances the method and tools of 3D nanoparticle analysis both theoretically and in application.

% This thesis provides an understanding and investigation into confined diffusion measurements.

The first section of the thesis describes a theoretical model of Brownian motion based on the confined-Langevin model using a Debye-Huckel electrostatic potential. The model was implemented, based on literature \cite{millanNumericalSimulationsConfined2023}, into a computer-based Python simulation in order to parameterize and create prior distributions of particle motion. 

The second section describes a modified method of 3D tracking to obtain faster calculations of particle position from optical microscopy images. This was based on an algorithm described in literature \cite{dewitPreciseCharacterizationNanometerscale2023}. The validation of the method was done on exact point-spread function simulation data with an accuracy of 1 nm.  

The third section describes the aquisition of  experimental data using a custom interferometric scattering microscope (iSCAT) with image pixel resolution 55 nm aquiring images between 400 and 1000 frames per second. The data consisted of images of scattering fringes from nanoparticle species and was analyzed using the algorithm developed in section 2. The resultant information was accuracte 3D particle tracks. The models developed in sectino 1 were applied to the tracks to obtain infer particle size and charge. 

The thesis presents a method of finding particle size, motion, and electrostatics which can present additional tools to analyze nanoparticle characterization, which was the goal of the thesis.
