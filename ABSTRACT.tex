\section*{Abstract}


The aim of this thesis is to characterize the full diffusion model of complex nanoparticles. To accomplish this, the 3D position of a nanoparticle(20-100 nm) must be measureable with a time resolution on the other of the diffusion length (ms).  Interferometric light scattering microscopy (iSCAT) is used  in tandem with convex-lens induced confinement (CLiC) to simultaneously confine particles, while obtaining a spatial signal. 

This thesis developed a novel method of measuring axial position using iSCAT data. The method was applied to a hard-wall axial confinement potential. The system's ionic conditions, particle charge, and confinement length were varied to characterize the effect of the various variables. 

The application of this thesis was directly applied to a more common, commericially assembled fluorescent microscope. The applications of the iScat confinement measurements allow for a direct correction to fluorescet microscopy calculations of diffusion in confinemnt. To accomplish this, an off-the-shelf optical system was designed. The system consists of a 3-color laser excitation with alternating laser excitation capabilities. The Nikon Ti2-Eclipse microscope chassis was used for this system in tandem with a 2-camera twincam system for dual color imaging.  Imaging of nanoparticles was performed under various confinement regimes and corrections to the measuements was performed.
