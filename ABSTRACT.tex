\section*{Abstract}


% intro
Particle confinement allows extended measurements of suspended biomolecules and nanoparticles using optical microscopy as it keeps it within the field of view.

% problem
However, during confinement, the size-diffusivity relationship changes from that in bulk, where the correction factor is typically assumed without consideration of the occurring measurement conditions such as surface charges and the media. 

%statement
In this work, we show that by combining interferometric scattering (iSCAT) microscopy with microfluidic nanoparticle confinement we can directly measure the positional distribution and depth-dependent diffusivity. We demonstrate how it depends on particle charge and media properties for both reference beads and lipid nanoparticles. Utilizing range-of-depth motion and in-plane diffusivity as complementary diameter estimates, we showcase how 3D particle tracking under confinement allows for nanoparticle diameter quantification in protein-containing media.

% conclusion/outlook
The ability to measure the effect of confined diffusivity improves accuracy in trajectory-based particle sizing in varying media, commonly found in cell biology and nanoparticle sizing.\cite{kamanziSimultaneousSingleParticleMeasurements2021}.
