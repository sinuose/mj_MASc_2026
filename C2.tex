\subsection{Apparatus schematic}

The schematic of the system is shown in  Fig \ref{fig:schematic} to remind the reader of the system. The optical system used for imaging is an iSCAT microscope. 

The optical system consisted of a $\SI{405}{\nm}$ excitation laser. The output of the laser was $\SI{240}{\milli \watt}$ pre-spatial filter, with an output of $\SI{120}{\milli \watt}$ post-spatial filter. The objective used was the $100 \times$ oil imaging objective (Nikon, Apochromat TIRF $100 \times$ oil immersion objective N.A. 1.49, W.D. $\SI{0.12}{\mm}$, F.O.V $\SI{22}{\mm}$). The camera used was the (\textcolor{red}{PRODUCT ID}) CMOS camera. Operating software was custom written by (\textcolor{red}{Author}) in LabView.

The flow-cell device consisted of two glass substrates (Ted Pella, product no.260372, $145 \pm \SI{15}{\micro \meter}$ thick, $25 \times \SI{25}{\mm}$ cover glass), separated by a 30 um thick double-sided adhesive spacer (Nitto-Denko, product no. 5603). 

\subsection{Convex-lens induced confinement}

The CLiC system was used to achieve nanometer confinement while imaging. This method is described in detail in the seminal publication \cite{berardPrecisionPlatformConvex2013}. A convex pusher lens is used together with a piezo linear actuator to displace the top coverslip of the flow-cell in a parabolic displacement. The piezo actuator is manually controlled during imaging and set to a specific, controlled height. The height is determined using the inference fringes of Newton's rings. The full method outline is visualized in Fig. \ref{fig:c7_fig1}

\section{Results}

% mission statement
A series of experiments were conducted to determine the particle size and to validate mathematical framework presented. 

To investigate effects of varied electrostatic interactions, the electrostatic screening length was varied using three concentrations of phosphate buffered saline solution. The nanoparticle used was $\SI{100}{\nm}$ carboxylate-modified polystyrene beads.  

To investigate the diffusivity profile of transient motion, the viscosity of the media was increased to obtain high-resolution axial trajectories. A solution of 75\% glycerol with 25\% 1x PBS was used to measure slow-diffusion of $\SI{100}{\nm}$ carboxylate-modified polystyrene beads at a frame rate of $\SI{1000}{\fps}$. 

To investigate the effects of confinement on lipid nanoparticles, three formulations of LNP were imaged using a two-step passivation technique. The three-dimensional position was measured to investigate axial movement in complex media. 

\subsection{Polystyrene PBS series}

The effect of ionic concentration on 100 nm polystyrene beads were performed. The beads were suspended in a series of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) solutions with varying ionic concentrations. Ten videos were obtained according to the method described in \ref{sec:methods}. The resulting videos allowed to measure the three-dimensional particle position in a confined height of $\SI{220}{\nm}$. The positional probability density of particle motion is  in Fig. \ref{fig:c7_fig3}. 

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.8\textwidth]{Python/Figures/Chapter7/c7_fig3.pdf}
    \caption{The positional histogram of particle position is plotted for three concentrations of PBS. Violet has a concentration of 0.1\% PBS, green has a concentration of 0.01\% PBS, and blue has a concentration of 0.00001\% PBS. The horizontal axis is the axial ($z$-axis) position of the particle. The vertical axis is the frequency of position measurement.}
    \label{fig:c7_fig3}
\end{figure}

Trace blue and green have the same distribution. The difference between peaks is normalization artifact. Trace violet does not following the same distribution, but rather is flat. The diffusion in blue and green is screened, where the particle does not explore the full height. In violet the particle does explore the well more. As the concentration of PBS increases, the distribution becomes more uniform, while in the higher concentrations, the particle is focused between the two plates. 

This experiment agrees with the simulations done as shown in Fig. \ref{fig:brown_realization}.
