\subsection{Apparatus schematic}

The schematic of the system is shown in  Fig \ref{fig:schematic} to remind the reader of the system. The optical system used for imaging is an iSCAT microscope. 

The optical system consisted of a $\SI{405}{\nm}$ excitation laser. The output of the laser was $\SI{240}{\milli \watt}$ pre-spatial filter, with an output of $\SI{120}{\milli \watt}$ post-spatial filter. The objective used was the $100 \times$ oil imaging objective (Nikon, Apochromat TIRF $100 \times$ oil immersion objective N.A. 1.49, W.D. $\SI{0.12}{\mm}$, F.O.V $\SI{22}{\mm}$). The camera used was the (\textcolor{red}{PRODUCT ID}) CMOS camera. Operating software was custom written by (\textcolor{red}{Author}) in LabView. 

\begin{figure}[t]
	\centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Figures/schematic.pdf}

\subsection{Cover-Glass}

The flow-cell device consisted of two glass substrates (Ted Pella, product no.260372, $145 \pm \SI{15}{\micro \meter}$ thick, $25 \times \SI{25}{\mm}$ cover glass), separated by a 30 um thick double-sided adhesive spacer (Nitto-Denko, product no. 5603). 

\subsection{Convex-lens induced confinement}

The CLiC system was used to achieve nanometer confinement while imaging. This method is described in detail in the seminal publication \cite{berardPrecisionPlatformConvex2013}. A convex pusher lens is used together with a piezo linear actuator to displace the top coverslip of the flow-cell in a parabolic displacement. The piezo actuator is manually controlled during imaging and set to a specific, controlled height. The height is determined using the inference fringes of Newton's rings. The full method outline is visualized in Fig. \ref{fig:c7_fig1}

\section{Results of iSCAT experiments}

% mission statement
A series of experiments were conducted to use the mathmatical method presented in the previous section to find the nanoparticle size and compare it to a known value of $100$ nm carboxylate-modified polystyrene beads. Effects of electrostatic interactions were investigated using piranha-cleaned borosilicate glass coverslips and phosphate-buffered saline solution (PBS). The diffusivity profile of transient motion was investgated using glycerol-water solution to modify the viscosity of the media.  A solution of 75\% glycerol with 25\% 1x PBS was used to measure transient-diffusion effects of $\SI{100}{\nm}$ carboxylate-modified polystyrene beads at a frame rate of $\SI{1000}{\fps}$. The effects of confinement on lipid nanoparticles, three formulations of lipid nanoparticle were imaged using iSCAT. To prevent sticking to the glass coverslips, a two-step poly-ethelyene glycol passivation technique was used. 

\section{Electrostatics Effects on Polystyrene Bead Diffusion in PBS}
\chaptermark{Elec. Eff. on PS. Bead Diff. in PBS}

The effect of ionic concentration on 100 nm polystyrene beads were performed. The beads were suspended in a series of phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) solutions with varying ionic concentrations. Ten videos were obtained according to the method described in \ref{sec:methods}. The videos are large ($\sim \SI{30}{\gb}$ per video) and are contained in a zip file upon request. 

For each measurement, $\SI{100}{\nm}$ diameter beads were confined into a $\SI{220}{\nm}$ slit verified using inference fringes. The output from each video was a collection of trajectories. For each concentration, the trajectories were reconstructed in three-dimensional space. The number of particles for each concentration of PBS are listed; $0.1\%, 8$, $0.01\%, 53$, $0.001\%, 13$, $0.00001\%, 62$. To visualize the trajectory density for different ionic concentrations, the trajectories of the diffusion are combined into density plots. 

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Python/Figures/density.pdf}
    \caption{Spatial density of the average trajectory of particles. }
    \label{fig:trajectory_ensemble}
\end{figure}


The resulting videos allowed to measure the three-dimensional particle position in a confined height of $\SI{220}{\nm}$. The positional probability density of particle motion is  in Fig. \ref{fig:c7_fig3}. 

\begin{figure}[h!]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Python/Figures/Chapter7/c7_fig3.pdf}
    \caption{Probability density for three different concentrations of PBS. Blue has a concentration of $0.1\%$ PBS, green has a concentration of $0.01\%$ PBS, and orange has a concentration of $0.00001\%$ PBS. The horizontal axis is the axial ($z$-axis) position of the particle. The vertical axis is the frequency of position measurement.}
    \label{fig:c7_fig3}
\end{figure}

Trace blue and green have the same distribution. The difference between peaks is normalization artifact. Trace violet does not following the same distribution, but rather is flat. The diffusion in blue and green is screened, where the particle does not explore the full height. In violet the particle does explore the well more. As the concentration of PBS increases, the distribution becomes more uniform, while in the higher concentrations, the particle is focused between the two plates. This experiment agrees with the simulations done as shown in Fig. \ref{fig:brown_realization}. The individual $\mathrm{FWHM}$ of this distribution is plotted compared to the PBS concentration. This is shown in Fig. \ref{fig:FWHM_PBS}.

\section{Polystyrene Transient Diffusion}

To investigate if transient diffusion information can be obtained with this method, polystyrene beads were once again used. The beads were suspended in 45\% glycerol and 1x phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) solutions. Ten videos, of length 10000 frames each, were obtained according to the method described in \ref{sec:methods}. The videos are large ($\sim \SI{30}{\gb}$ per video) and are contained in a zip file upon request. 

The glycerol was adjusted to ensure that the diffusion coefficient was around $10\times$ smaller so that the mean step length was $\sim \SI{10}{\nm}$. This was done using the glycerol-water weight curve. This curve is shown in  Fig. \ref{fig:gly_curve}.

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Python/Figures/glycerol_curve.pdf}
    \caption{glycerol-water curve. The curve is taken from \cite{cheng_formula_2008}.}
    \label{fig:gly_curve}
\end{figure}


The mean-squared displacement of the transverse and axial motion is plotted in Fig. \ref{fig:gly_msd}. 

\begin{figure}[h]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Python/Figures/glycerol_MSD.pdf}
    \caption{Mean-squared displacement of glycerol-slowed polystyrene beads. Axial MSD is clearly convergent to the confinement length. The confinement length is fixed at $225$ nm and the particle's estimated size is $a=\SI{50}{\nm}$.}

\end{figure}
