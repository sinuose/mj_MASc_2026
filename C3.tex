\section{Simulation Equations}

The simulation scheme follows that descirbed in \cite{millanNumericalSimulationsConfined2023}. A brownian particle diffuses between two walls such that $(x,y)$ are perpendicular to the walls, and $z$ is parallel. The electrostatic force that the particle feels is then only in the $z$ direction. The diffusivity of the particle will depend on the distance of the walls. The langevin equation simulated, 
\begin{equation}
    m\ddot{\vec{x}(t)} = - \lambda \dot{\vec{x}(t)} + \vec F(\vec{x}(t)) + \sqrt{2k_b T \lambda} \vec{W}(t),
\end{equation}
is first seperated into transverse and axial components, where $F$ is the electrostatic force, $\lambda$ is the friction. Here, we can re-write the diffusion coefficient using the Stokes-Einstein relation,
\begin{equation}
    D(z) = \frac{k_b T}{\lambda(z)}.
\end{equation}

Then, in overdamped regime, where $m \ddot{\vec{x}} \ll 0$, we can write the equations of motion as
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        \frac{dx}{dt} &= \sqrt{2D_x(z)} W_x(t) \\
        \frac{dz}{dt} &= \sqrt{2D_z(z)} W_z(t) + \frac{F_z(z)}{\lambda(z)},
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
as descirbed in Eqn. 7 of \cite{millanNumericalSimulationsConfined2023}. This is exceptionally valid for particles between $\SI{10}{\nm} - \SI{100}{\micro \meter}$ as this is exactly the overdamped regime for many particle materials, such as PMMA or coated polystyrene.

To simulate this equation, we use an Euler-Maruyama scheme. To illustrate this, we can rewrite the above equations in the Ito form, replacing lowercase variables with their captial, stochastic, equivalents:
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        dX_t &= \sqrt{2D_x(z)} dW_x \\
        dZ_t &= \sqrt{2D_z(z)} dW_z + \frac{F_z(z)}{\lambda(z)} dt,
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}

The simulated system of equations is then the pair of equations;
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        x_{n+1} &= x_n + \sqrt{2D_x(z_n)} W_{x,n}\Delta t\\
        z_{n+1} &= z_n + \left[D'_z(z_n) - \beta D_z(z_n) V'(z_n) + \sqrt{2D_z(z_n)} W_{x,n}\right]\Delta t\\
    \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $V(z)$ is the electrostatic potential, $\beta = 1/k_b T$, and $\Delta t$ is the discretized time. 

\section{Surface Charge Estimator}

As described earlier, the relations for the effective potential are described if one knows the particle properties and surface charge. In terms of measureables, electrostatic interactions can be fully described by the following  equation,

\begin{equation}
    U(z) = B\left[e^{-(H - z)/\lambda_D} - e^{-(H + z)/\lambda_D}\right],
\end{equation}
where the prefactor is described by the map $f: (\zeta_p, \sigma_w, a, \lambda_D, T)$,
\begin{align}
    B &= C \cdot \tanh\left(\frac{e_0 \zeta_p}{4k_b T}\right) \cdot \tanh\left(\frac{e_0 \psi_w}{4k_b T}\right) \\
    C & = \frac{64 \pi \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r a k_b T}{e_0^2}\\
    \psi_w &= \frac{2k_b T}{e_0}\text{asinh}\left[\frac{\sigma_w e_0 \lambda_D}{2 \varepsilon_0 \varepsilon_r k_b T}\right]
\end{align}
where $\zeta_p$ is the zeta-potential of the particle, $\psi_w$ is the stern-layer potenital for the surface walls, $a$ is the particle radius, $e_0$ is the elementary charge value, and $\lambda_D$ is the debye-length.
